\section{Discussion and Threats to Validity}\label{sec:limits}
\subsection{What source evidence establishes}
The analysis demonstrates that selected source paths expose distinctions that can be inspected and tested. It does not show that all deployments exercise them correctly. Database configuration, timer behavior, clock discipline, adapter fidelity, and callers that omit optional ownership arguments can alter practical guarantees. A full safety audit would need to enumerate and verify every relevant transition, including cancellation, restart, terminal cleanup, and project isolation. No formal refinement proof connects the entire implementation to Equation~\ref{eq:projection}.

Provenance checks protect a narrower property than substantive correctness. A source can be faithfully transported and still be false, irrelevant, or misunderstood. Selection can omit necessary evidence. A schema-valid completion decision may embody an incorrect acceptance judgment. This is why evidence transport, dependency compliance, and independent task quality are different evaluation dimensions. More logging alone would not resolve these distinctions.

\subsection{Historical records and selection bias}
The retained seven-role case is a historical process record. Its evidence ledger records overlap between analysts and creation of downstream roles before synthesizer success. The foundation audit documents that the fact checker considered early material and that an initial typed publication path did not complete before a later publication path produced the report. The underlying historical database is not part of the retained artifact, so exact locator replay cannot be independently repeated from this package alone. We use the record as motivation for a failure mode, not as current-version failure evidence or as an evaluated workflow success.

Other project materials contain website benchmark constants without the complete versioned dataset, evaluator, per-case outputs, or repeated-run records needed for scientific interpretation. Those numbers do not appear in our result table or effect calculations. A maintainer-assisted Actual case is likewise a selected demonstration with intervention and repair history, not independent adoption evidence. The existence of a final report, patch, or public issue does not turn these materials into a random sample.

Historical foundation acceptance receipts describe their own stage and review state. They are not a certification of this manuscript. We preserve those receipts rather than rewriting them to imply present scientific validation. Any future reproduction must retain raw failures as well as eventual successes.

\subsection{Internal, construct, and external validity}
Interface adaptation is a principal internal-validity risk. A baseline might receive a weaker tool schema, less favorable context handling, or less tuning. Equal nominal token caps do not ensure equal usable context. We therefore require visible adapters, disjoint development tasks, aggregate accounting, and intervention records. Calendar-time model updates and provider nondeterminism remain possible even with a fixed catalog name; request identity and observation date are necessary replication information.

The acceptance oracle is a construct-validity risk. Passing tests may miss requested behavior, while a rubric may reward presentation rather than correctness. A recovery status may describe availability rather than safe continuation. Our separate endpoint definitions reduce these conflations but do not eliminate incomplete or biased evaluation. Reviewer blinding may be imperfect because output formats reveal the runtime. Initial ratings, disagreement, adjudication, and hidden-test coverage should be disclosed.

External validity is limited by a single proposed model, selected software workloads, and the specific infrastructure configuration. Repository clustering and public-task contamination further restrict generalization. None of these experiments, even if successful, would by itself establish broad community adoption, economic value, self-improvement across tasks, or correctness of arbitrary autonomous agents. Those claims need separate evidence and study designs.

\subsection{Operational implications and open questions}
The design suggests a practical diagnostic order: identify the business revision being evaluated, identify the exact execution occurrence, inspect current ownership at the transition, and then examine the artifact versions actually consumed. This is a procedure for locating evidence, not an automated guarantee of diagnosis. Where external outcomes are unknown, the runtime must expose uncertainty to an authorized decision maker rather than convert it into a fabricated success.

Several questions remain open. Do explicit reference and selection steps impose enough interaction cost to offset their diagnostic value? Do periodic scans remain responsive under heavy multi-project contention? Which kinds of task benefit from separating recorded acceptance from an independent oracle? The proposed ablations and fault scenarios address parts of these questions; only their results can support a practical effectiveness claim.

\section{Conclusion}
We have presented a source-grounded analysis of \oc{} that distinguishes business acceptance, execution occurrences, physical ownership, and revision-specific evidence. Under explicit assumptions, selected mechanisms support stale-identity rejection at guarded database transitions, closure over declared source references, and eventual opportunities to rediscover eligible work. Counterexamples establish the limits of these statements: local durability does not imply exactly-once external effects, reference closure does not imply correct reasoning, and final delivery does not imply dependency-order compliance.

The accompanying protocol defines how to evaluate recovery, independently accepted outcomes, and mechanism costs without conflating them. \pending{Whether these mechanisms improve outcomes relative to a matched runtime, and at what cost, remains unanswered until the proposed experiments are performed.} This is a complete non-experimental working argument, with empirical sections explicitly reserved. It is not a submission-ready empirical paper.
